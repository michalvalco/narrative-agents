# Narrative Agents: Community Engagement Playbook

## Common Questions & Your Responses

### "How is this different from RAG?"
**Your Response:**
"Great question! RAG retrieves relevant documents from a database—it's still storage-based retrieval. Narrative agents interpret experiences through their purpose (telos) and selectively form identity from them. 

Think of it this way: RAG is like having a better filing system. Narrative agents are like developing a personality. 

RAG asks: 'What's relevant to this query?'
Narrative agents ask: 'What's constitutive to who I'm becoming?'

The results speak for themselves—70% less memory with higher behavioral coherence."

---

### "Isn't this just pruning with extra steps?"
**Your Response:**
"I had the same skepticism initially! But pruning is random or recency-based deletion. This is interpretive selection through narrative coherence.

The key difference: our agents developed actual character traits (resilience, excellence) that influenced future decisions. Traditional pruning doesn't create emergent identity.

Run the code yourself—watching agents diverge from identical experiences is... well, it's sobering."

---

### "What about hallucination risks?"
**Your Response:**
"Actually, this might reduce hallucination. Here's why:

Traditional agents hallucinate partly because they lose narrative coherence—they forget who they're supposed to be. Narrative agents maintain identity even with limited memory.

That said, you raise an important point about failure modes. What happens when an agent forms a harmful narrative identity? That's precisely what I'm researching next. Want to collaborate?"

---

### "Seems computationally expensive"
**Your Response:**
"Counterintuitively, it's more efficient! The narrative core is bounded by design. No more fighting context window overflow with hacks.

The interpretive layer is just a function call—negligible overhead. And you're storing 70% less while getting better behavior.

The real cost savings come from not needing massive context windows or complex retrieval systems."

---

### "Can this scale to production?"
**Your Response:**
"Fair concern. The architecture is designed for scale:
- Bounded memory (no linear growth)
- Simple interpretive functions
- No complex retrieval needed

We're running trials with 10K+ experiences now. Early results show coherence actually improves with scale as identity stabilizes.

What's your use case? Happy to discuss implementation details."

## Engagement Tactics by Platform

### Twitter/X

**High-value replies:**
- QT (quote tweet) with additional insights
- Reply with a specific code snippet
- Share a surprising result
- Ask a follow-up question

**Example QT:**
"This is exactly right. What's curious, however, is that forgetting isn't just about efficiency—it's constitutive to identity formation.

When our narrative agents 'forgot' 70% of experiences, they didn't lose coherence. They gained it.

Thread on why: [link]"

**Build relationships:**
- Follow everyone who engages meaningfully
- DM top engagers with exclusive content
- Create Twitter lists: "Narrative AI Thinkers"
- Host Spaces on Fridays

---

### LinkedIn

**Professional positioning:**

Start responses with validation:
- "Your experience in [their industry] gives you unique insight here..."
- "As someone who's wrestled with this in production, you'll appreciate..."

End with value:
- "I'm writing a detailed implementation guide—DM me if you'd like early access"
- "We're forming a working group on this—interested?"

**LinkedIn-specific tactics:**
- Connect with every meaningful commenter
- Send personalized connection notes referencing their comment
- Share their content with your take
- Endorse skills strategically

---

### Reddit

**Community rules:**
- Never directly promote in first response
- Provide value first, links second
- Acknowledge criticism genuinely
- Award good questions/critiques

**Response template:**
"That's a solid point about [concern]. In our implementation, we handled this by [solution].

Here's the specific code that addresses your concern: [code snippet]

The tricky part was [challenge]. We tried [failed approach] first, but [working solution] ended up being more robust.

What approach would you take?"

## The 80/20 of Community Building

**20% that drives 80% of value:**
1. Respond genuinely to skeptics (builds credibility)
2. Nurture 5 champions (they'll build your community)
3. Turn questions into content (infinite material)
4. Share credit generously (creates advocates)

**What to skip:**
- Arguing with trolls
- Perfect responses (authentic > polished)
- Chasing vanity metrics
- Paid promotion initially
